The craft · कारीगरी

१ / ४

१

Carve खुदाई

Every block begins as a seasoned slab of sheesham. The carver draws the motif in chalk and cuts it with fifty-odd chisels — a single paisley can take four days. Each colour needs its own block: an outline, the rekh, then its fills, the datta.

4 days · 52 chisels · 1 slab of sheesham

२

Dye रंगाई

Our colours are boiled, not bought: madder root for red, indigo vats for blue, pomegranate rind and turmeric for yellow, iron and jaggery for black. Dabu — a resist of black clay, gum and wheat — keeps the cloth pale where colour must not go.

Madder · Indigo · Pomegranate · Iron

३

Stamp छपाई

The printer dips the block in a tray of dye-soaked felt, sets it on the cloth by eye and strikes it once with the heel of the hand. Registration is done by feel — about four hundred impressions an hour, each one a little different.

400 impressions / hour · 0 rulers

४

Wash धुलाई

Prints are sun-dried, steamed, then washed in running water to lift the excess dye and clay. The cloth goes in muddy and comes out singing — and it softens with every wash after, yours included.

3 days in the sun · 1 river

The printer · छीपा

“The block remembers every hand that has held it. The cloth only remembers mine for a morning.”

Kailash-ji, master printer — fourth generation of a printing family in Bagru

Kailash learnt to print at nine, on the back verandah, from a grandfather who never once measured a repeat. Today he runs CHHAPA's table with twenty-three printers and nine carvers. They are paid by the month, not the metre — because a printer who is hurried makes a cloth that looks hurried.
