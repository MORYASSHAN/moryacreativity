Ichi-go ichi-e · one time, one meeting

The blossom lasts a week. We built the whole journey around that week.

Every spring the sakura zensen, the cherry-blossom front, moves north up the archipelago at roughly the pace of a person walking. Hanami travels with it. Seven castle towns, four family-run ryokan, one guide who has made this walk for nineteen springs, and enough empty hours to sit under a tree and do nothing at all.

壱 · Night 1

Tsukikage-an 月影庵

Six rooms around a moss garden in Matsumoto, run by the same family since 1911. Ask for the moon-viewing room; the bath faces the Alps.

Ryokan · Hinoki bath · Kaiseki

弐 · Day 5

Night boat on the moat 夜桜舟

A flat-bottomed boat poled slowly under lit cherry trees in Hikone, the castle above, warm sake below. The boatman sings if asked twice.

参 · Day 3

Dawn tea, before the gates 朝茶

A private tea ceremony in a Kanazawa garden pavilion at six in the morning, an hour before the first visitors, when the only sound is the stream.

肆 · Night 6

Kaiseki in the upper grove 吉野

A temple lodging on the ridge of Yoshino, where thirty thousand trees bloom in four bands up the mountain. Twelve courses, one long table, no rush.
