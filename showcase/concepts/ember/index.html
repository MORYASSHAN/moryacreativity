01 — The season, counted

Last summer the fire moved faster than the news.
These are the numbers we carry.

412,380

hectares burned

Across four valleys in a single season — an area the wind crossed in eleven days and the land will take thirty years to forget.

1,184

homes lost

Nine in ten stood within thirty metres of unmanaged brush. Most of that brush could have been cleared by twelve people with hand tools in a weekend.

6,420

volunteers

Trained, insured, fed, and — every single one of them — sent home safe. Teachers, mechanics, nurses, a retired pilot, two hundred students.

02 — How we help

Fire has three seasons. So do we.

Before

Clear & prepare

We cut firebreaks, thin fuel loads around homes and schools, and run door-to-door readiness drills in the villages the maps forget.

214 km of break cut last spring

During

Shelter & supply

When the evacuation call comes we are already there: go-bags, water, warm meals, animal crates, phone chargers, and someone who knows your name.

31,900 nights of shelter given

After

Rebuild & replant

Grants that arrive in days, not months. Volunteer crews for clean-up. And for every hectare lost, native seedlings raised in our community nurseries.

88,000 native seedlings planted

03 — Anatomy of a fire season

It never starts with the flames. It starts with the dry.

  1. April

    The dry arrives

    Rain stops early. Grass cures to gold, then to tinder. We train new crews and walk every firebreak.

  2. May

    Fuel moisture falls below 10%

    A dropped cigarette is now a forecast. We pre-position water tanks and go-bags in forty-two villages.

  3. July

    Ignition

    Dry lightning, a power line, a careless spark. The first column of smoke is visible from three valleys.

  4. August

    The front runs

    With wind behind it the fire moves six kilometres an hour. Evacuation calls go out. Our shelters open within ninety minutes.

  5. September

    Holding the line

    Brigades hold; volunteers feed them. We run supply lines, animal rescue and a hotline that never sleeps.

  6. October

    Rain. Ash. Counting.

    The first rain smells like a bonfire. Families return to what is left. We are at the gate with gloves and a plan.

  7. Nov — Mar

    Green, again

    Rebuild grants, mental-health circles, and seedlings in the ground before the next dry comes.
